# Problem Statement

## Background

Data centers depend on cooling infrastructure and can require substantial water and energy resources depending on their cooling architecture and operating conditions.

Water may be lost through evaporation, blowdown, leakage, and maintenance operations. Meanwhile, pumps, fans, moving water, vibration, and airflow can create low-grade energy sources.

## Core Problem

How can a small-scale integrated system investigate water recovery and low-grade waste-energy harvesting while continuously monitoring the relevant operating parameters?

## Objectives

1. Demonstrate a controlled water recovery and treatment pathway.
2. Measure hydraulic energy harvesting.
3. Measure vibration/piezoelectric energy harvesting.
4. Use an ESP32 for monitoring and control.
5. Quantify water and energy performance experimentally.
6. Document limitations and scalability considerations.

## Scope

The prototype is a controlled educational/research demonstration. It does not claim to provide certified industrial water treatment or replace data-center primary power.
